Durable Execution for Multi-Agent LLM Systems

Authors: J. Nakamura, L. Fischer, R. Iyer
Submitted: 2026-09-12 · Subject: cs.AI, cs.MA

Abstract

We study checkpointing strategies for long-running multi-agent LLM pipelines. We show that persisting a node's result before scheduling its successors bounds recovery time to a single node's execution rather than an entire run. Across three orchestration frameworks we observe a 34-71% reduction in re-executed model calls after a mid-run failure, at the cost of one extra write per node. We further show that per-tool rate limiting removes the retry storms that dominate token spend under flaky tool backends. Our results suggest that durability and admission control, not prompt engineering, are the dominant lever on agent operating cost.

Limitations

Evaluation is confined to three open-source frameworks and synthetic failure injection; we do not measure production workloads, and the retry-storm finding relies on a single tool backend with injected latency.
